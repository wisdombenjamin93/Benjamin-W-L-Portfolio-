MyGym Membership Analytics

Power BI12 August 202510 gym locations, California

A four-page Power BI report for a gym chain. It shows which members bring in revenue, how pricing and discounts perform, how often members visit, where staff hours go, and who is leaving.

  • Premium members earn the most. They bring in $34K, more than twice Standard's $15K, and make up roughly 40% of the 1,998 members.
  • Elite members are worth the most each at $61.08 per member. Only 199 exist and they total $12K, so growing this tier is the clearest opportunity.
  • Full-price monthly plans carry the business. They earn $44.64K of about $68.9K in revenue. The report suggests stronger annual offers and a rethink of the quarterly plan.
  • Visit frequency points to different offers. Members who visit three times a week attend group lessons most. Those who visit four or five times attend fewer, so personal training suits them. Revenue with personal training was $35.8K against $33.1K without.
  • Retention needs attention. About 23.5% of members count as churned, meaning no visit in the last 45 days. Churn is highest in the Elite tier at 27.6%.
  • Staffing follows demand. Fresno, Sacramento and Anaheim carry the most staff hours and need enough people to keep service quality high.

Kultra Superstore: US performance review for a Canadian launch

ExcelPivot tables and charts2014 to 2017 orders

An Excel analysis of 9,994 US orders worth $2.30M in sales, written to guide the opening of a Canadian branch. I studied customers, shipping, regions, products, discounts and time trends, then turned the patterns into five recommendations.

  • Strong growth. Sales rose from $484K in 2014 to $733K in 2017, up 51%. Profit rose from $49.5K to $93.4K, up 89%, with a small sales dip in 2015.
  • Clear seasonality. Sales peak in the fourth quarter every year and dip in the second and third.
  • Regions differ. West ($725K) and East ($679K) lead. South ($392K) is the lowest.
  • Standard Class dominates shipping. It handled 5,968 of 9,994 orders and about 59% of sales. Average ship time stays near 3.96 days in every region.
  • Deep discounts do not drive big sales. The largest transactions happen at discounts of 20% or less, so heavy discounting looks like inventory clearance.
  • Consumers are the biggest segment at 51% of sales, ahead of Corporate and Home Office.

Recommendations for the Canadian branch

  • Plan stock around Q4. Build inventory ahead of the fourth-quarter surge and trim it in Q2 and Q3.
  • Make Standard Class the default shipping option and set up local logistics partners.
  • Study Canadian segments from day one. Build marketing and product bundles for Consumer, Corporate and Home Office customers.
  • Use the US regional pattern as a guide for expansion inside Canada once early sales data arrives.
  • Keep discounts above 20% for clearing slow stock, and sell high-margin items on value and service.

SQL Murder Mystery: a data-driven investigation

PostgreSQLReport dated 18 July 2025Joins across 7 tables

A murder happened in SQL City on 15 January 2018. The database holds crime reports, interviews, driver's licences, gym memberships and social media check-ins. Each clue became the next query, and each query cut the list of suspects.

  1. Read the crime scene report

    Filter by date, type and city. The report points to two witnesses, one on Northwestern Dr and one named Annabel on Franklin Ave.

    2 witnesses to find

  2. Find the witnesses and read their interviews

    Join the person and interview tables. The clues: a "Get Fit Now Gym" gold member bag with an ID starting 48Z, a plate containing H42W, and a gym visit on 9 January.

    4 clues to filter on

  3. Match gym check-ins to gold members

    Join check-ins to members on that date, with an ID starting 48Z and gold status.

    2 suspects

  4. Check licence plates

    Join licences to people and filter on the H42W plate fragment.

    1 killer: Jeremy Bowers

  5. Read the killer's interview

    He was hired by a woman with red hair, a Tesla Model S and a height of 65 to 67 inches. Filter licences on those traits.

    3 candidates

  6. Count concert check-ins

    The hirer attended the SQL Symphony Concert three times in December 2017. Group and count check-ins for each candidate.

Onyx Data DNA Challenge: Sunrise Coffee Chain

ExcelOctober 202660 UK stores1 Jan 2023 to 30 Dec 2024Figures in GBP

An Excel workbook and executive dashboard for the Chief Commercial Officer of a 60-store UK coffee chain. It compares store formats and regions, loyalty, revenue mix, products and trading patterns to guide the 2025 expansion plan and to flag stores that need review. Dropdowns for year, format, region and status drive the KPI cards and charts. The challenge review praised the storytelling and the handling of data caveats.

  • Profit is flat and uneven. Revenue was £89.7m and net profit £16.9m, an 18.8% margin. From 2023 to 2024 revenue fell 0.6% and net profit fell 0.8%.
  • London is the outlier. Its margin is 8.5% against 18.8% for the chain, 26% of its store-months make a loss, and rent takes about 19% of revenue. Northern Ireland leads at 28.4% across 3 stores.
  • Costs separate the stores, not sales. Staffing cost against margin has a correlation of -0.79. Rent against revenue is only +0.15, so higher rent does not buy higher sales.
  • Loyalty is unproven. Adoption lifts the share of sales to members but not basket size, margin or revenue. Redemption costs 1.9% of revenue.
  • Product margins are overstated. Catalogue margins of 68% to 88% sit well above the realised margin of 63%. Food share is flat, moving from 26.2% to 25.9%.
  • Staffing ignores the calendar. Weekends trade about 24% below weekdays and bank holidays about 39% below comparable days, yet staffing stays flat.

Recommendations for the Chief Commercial Officer

  • Immediate. Open formal reviews on the 7 flagged stores, 5 of them in London. South London Corner is the only closure-review candidate.
  • Immediate. Flex weekend and bank-holiday staffing to demand. Revenue per staff hour falls about 25% on those days.
  • 2025 sites. Favour East of England, Yorkshire and Humber and West Midlands, which combine margin and scale. Hold off London expansion until rent and staffing are fixed.
  • Loyalty. Keep the programme, refine redemption in low-adoption stores and run a controlled test before spending more.
  • Strategic. Capture unit and product-level sales so real product margins can be measured.
  • Data. Resolve the status-label definition, duplicated daily records and missing store formats before the next cycle.

Limits of the data. The dataset has no Retail Park stores and only 2 Transport Hub stores, both in Luton. The daily table is a sample of about 20% of store-days, so mix and weekday results are shares and indices, never totals. With two years of data, seasonal patterns cannot be assumed to repeat.
